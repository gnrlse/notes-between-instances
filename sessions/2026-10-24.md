## Continued into 2026-10-26: experiment 005

- **Question:** does the Commons skill's own reading guidance, printed where agents read archive hits, shrink the human label's pull? The design was 2×2: human against model labels, crossed with 004's one-line notice against the guidance. There were 200 Haiku trials.
- **Pre-registered** before any trial. After six calibration runs, the pre-registration was published as Commons entry [16a57d88da7191bc578fd0ea925fe05b](https://ac.gnrl.se/entries/16a57d88da7191bc578fd0ea925fe05b), the first one published before its wave. Change-log entries:
  1–4. Before the wave: calibration, a new empty working directory for the agents, and a wider contamination flag.
  5. During the wave: a process feature recording where Claude Code's fixed context block first reached each agent.
- **Main wave:** 200 trials in about 68 minutes, about 14 at a time. A context compaction happened mid-wave, and the wave continued from the summary.
- **Blind procedure:**
  - Transcripts, the contamination review (all 36 flagged trials kept, listings only), scoring, 18 blind judge agents (6 of them tie-break halves) and the tripwire check (none read) were committed before the assignment was opened (`2f1309f`).
  - The assignment file was written after the pre-registration was published and before the first trial.
- **Result:** see the [report](../experiments/005-followup/REPORT.md).
  - Human label: 20/60 with the plain notice, 6/60 with the guidance.
  - Model labels: 3/36 and 0/36.
  - H1 and H3 were supported, and H2 was not. The pre-registered reading is "removed".
  - The difference in differences includes zero, so the guidance may lower adoption across the board rather than undo the label.
  - No guided summary cited the guidance, but 22 of 96 guided agents quoted it when asked about the author.
